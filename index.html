Executive summary

Make openness visible, normal, and worth doing.

The first RERITE report should not read like a compliance manual. It should make one idea obvious: artifact availability is the practical bridge between scientific integrity and transportation practice.

Primary recommendation. Start with structured availability statements, persistent artifact links, basic link verification, and visible credit. Save full reproduction requirements for pilots, badges, student projects, and high-impact benchmarks.
State of the field

The central gap is contribution, not awareness.

Many transportation papers benefit from open datasets. Far fewer contribute processed data, code, models, or workflows back to the field.

Stakeholder audit dashboard

Use different statistics for different decisions.

The final report should not make every reader infer what matters to them. These are report-ready statistics from the MOST preprint, organized by decision role.

Annual report

RERITE's story is a flywheel: measure, guide, train, recognize, repeat.

The origin story should be brief and visual: a volunteer group formed because transportation researchers were repeatedly losing time rebuilding what should have been reusable.

Roadmap

The journey is from hidden work to translation-ready evidence.

This roadmap is a RERITE synthesis, not a single canonical standard. It crosswalks open-science policy, FAIR data, computational reproducibility, artifact review, replication, and translation-readiness frameworks.

Neighboring fields

Transportation can borrow policy patterns without copying another field whole.

The useful comparison is not "who is better." It is which policy mechanisms are already normal somewhere else.

Government funders

Funders are shifting the default from access to stewardship.

The global policy pattern is clear enough for transportation to act now: make outputs findable, require plans, budget curation, and explain justified restrictions.

Centerfold

Guidelines should be memorable enough to repeat in a hallway.

These are deliberately short. A fuller RERITE Guidelines v0.1 can put templates and examples behind them.

Editorial features

Let columns add voice without taking over the report.

Each column can grow to roughly 1000 words, but the reader should open one column directly from the card, skim the spread, and keep moving.

Behind the scenes

How the audit turns a paper corpus into a public field signal.

This section should give readers confidence without asking them to become methodologists. The annual audit is designed to be lightweight, repeatable, source-traceable, and honest about where automated measurement still needs human judgment.

Future work and reader feedback

The next metrics should be chosen with the community, not guessed in isolation.

These are candidate extensions beyond the January 2026 MOST preprint. Some are cheap enough for the 2026 release; others should become scoped pilots only if journals, funders, reviewers, and researchers say they would use the results.

People and credit

Open science is infrastructure, and infrastructure has people behind it.

The annual report should make contributor labor visible while keeping names easy to refresh before release.

Source appendix

Keep sources accessible, but out of the visual flow.

The report should archive policy pages where possible because conference and journal instructions change quickly.
